About this Project

This research explores the Birthday Paradox, its combinatorial roots, probabilistic reasoning, and real-world implications in cryptography and hashing. The site provides exact calculations, visual explanations, and interactive experiments.

Why intuition fails

The paradox grows from pair-counting: with n people there are n(n−1)/2 pairs, not n comparisons to a fixed date. That combinatorial explosion quickly raises collision chances.

For example, with just 23 people there are 253 unique pairs — each an independent opportunity for a birthday collision. The probability accumulates far faster than our linear intuition predicts.

Mathematical reasoning

The exact probability that at least two people in a group of n share a birthday:

P(n) = 1 − ∏k=0n−1 (365 − k) / 365

We implement precise calculations and Monte Carlo verification in the Simulation page.

Poisson Approximation: P(n) ≈ 1 − e−n(n−1)/(2×365)

Timeline

  • 1930s — Combinatorics foundations laid by Richard von Mises
  • 1950s — Puzzle popularized in probability textbooks
  • 1990s — Cryptography applications: birthday attack on hash functions
  • 2020s — Interactive simulations & educational platforms

Key Insight

The paradox isn't about matching your birthday — it's about any two people matching. The number of pairs grows quadratically: O(n²)
